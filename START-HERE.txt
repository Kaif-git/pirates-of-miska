PIRATES OF MISKA - SHIP LIFE

Launch: double-click Play Pirates.bat, or run python play.py from this folder.
Install dependencies if needed: python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
Fresh generated world: python play.py --new
Repeatable generation: python play.py --new --seed "your-seed"
Configure generated NPCs: python play.py --new --seed "your-seed" --npc-people 32 --npc-organizations 5 --npc-vessels 10 --history-days 30

New captains start aboard the Wayward, a moored sloop with a working deck,
forecastle/helm, mess, galley, water cask, cargo hold, workshop and six bunks.
The west gangplank connects the ship to the harbor. Port supplies are at (31,23).
Older saves keep their captain and inventory, and gain the new ship/crew systems.

PORT SUPPLIES / TRADING
Verdant Cay has farms, spring water, fisheries, timber and weaving. Cinder Quay
has foundries and manufacturers but imports provisions, water, timber and cloth.
Each island has three named merchants with different specialties and conversations.
Follow the west gangplank ashore. $ marks a merchant; stand within two tiles and
press Enter -> Talk / trade with their name. Merchant locations are in Enter ->
Island guide / coastal passage. Start with 600 Marks; funds persist across saves.

Verdant: Ada (30,21) sells crops; Bo (35,27) fish/water; Lina (25,28) wood/cloth.
Cinder: Dara buys provisions; Brann sells tools/metal/arms; Petra sells practical kit.
Their stalls use the same market coordinates on the respective islands.
Regional ask/bid prices depend on supply and demand. Bulk quotes include marginal
price changes. Merchants have finite stocks, budgets and accepted item categories.
Daily local production replenishes exports to a cap; local demand consumes imports.
Buying and reselling at the same merchant loses money. Regional trade can profit,
but passage, delivery, cargo capacity and changing prices must be considered.

Purchases go to TRADE CARGO via market porters: 2 Marks and 1 game minute per order.
This porter delivery is abstracted, not a simulated dock-labor job. Selling takes
10 seconds. Every transaction requires confirmation and rejects partial failures.
Trade cargo plus issued equipment has a 250 kg / 350 L limit, separate from the
captain's satchel and allocated working stockpiles. Goods, purses and history save.

Enter -> Trade cargo / crew equipment opens the manifest. Stand next to the ship's
hold to allocate provisions, issue/return kit or transfer goods to/from the satchel.
Allocated food becomes ingredients or ready meals; water fills its actual stockpile;
wood and shot enter actual working supplies, respecting available pile capacity.
Allocated supplies are no longer sale cargo. Material masses follow ship-storage units.

USEFUL ITEMS
Food: grain/beans/salted fish feed cooking; fruit/biscuits provide ready portions.
Water: sealed kegs provide ten servings each.
Wood: timber/plank bundles supply repairs and construction.
Tools: carpenter rolls +20% repair work; cookware +18% cooking; buckets +20% bailing;
mops +15% cleaning; issued rope +12% rigging.
Clothing: linen work clothes reduce work fatigue 15%; oilskins reduce it 25%;
boots reduce walking energy expenditure 35%. Weather/armor protection is not simulated.
Weapons: issue cutlasses/pistols and run arms drills with a rested, unoccupied sailor.
Sailors walk to the workshop and gain practice only while actually training; urgent
needs interrupt. Pistol practice consumes a standardized ammunition charge. Shipboard
boarding fights are available at pirate-voyage sightings. Iron, coal and sailcloth are
trade commodities, not new recipes.
Crew kit appears in F2 profiles. Issuing/replacing kit takes ten seconds; old items
return to cargo rather than being deleted. Trade goods are standard undamaged items.

ISLAND PASSAGE
At the harbor office (42,23) or beside the helm, Enter -> Island guide / coastal
passage books the other island for 15 Marks and 30 game minutes. The crew continues
working and consuming supplies. This is charted passage abstraction, NOT ship-handling
or naval sailing simulation. Ship geometry, fixtures, crew and cargo persist; each
harbor retains its own ground items, door states and exploration memory.

SEA VOYAGES / OFFICER ROLES
Press V for Voyage Command. The captain chooses a chart destination; the navigator
plots a lane and estimates time from rigging skill and wind. The quartermaster
reports water, prepared meals and raw ingredients against the route estimate,
warns about shortages/low hull and suggests provisioning/trade voyages. A separate,
numbers-minded purser tracks the stores ledger. V >
Assign roles lets you choose different named crew members for navigator,
quartermaster and purser. Officer roles influence reports/route estimates; they
do not replace the existing daily ship jobs.

Review the route and confirm the captain's order to depart. The left harbor becomes
open sea and the sea chart shows the Wayward (@), planned lanes (*) and rival ships.
Use V > Watch / advance the voyage for 10 minutes, 1 hour or 6 hours. Sailing legs
are strategic, abstract time steps, not continuous ship steering. The ordinary
ShipLife simulation still runs each second: crew needs, chores, fatigue, stores,
repairs and wear continue while the player watches the chart or walks the ship.
Arrival and nearby-vessel sightings interrupt a long watch. At a pirate sighting,
V > Resolve nearby-ship sighting > Repel a pirate boarding party stages a raid on
the built lower deck. Raiders open a real outer-hull breach, route toward the captain,
and force locked doors only when no unlocked alternate route exists. Victory resumes
the voyage; defeat or withdrawal orders a retreat. The breach, flooding, destroyed
doors and lockdown remain ship state. Other sightings still offer hold-course/turn-back.

V > Captain free-time action: rest (reduces captain fatigue), study charts (chart
notes improve the next voyage estimate), inspect the ship, or talk with crew.
V > Crew team order: work as needed, rest between watches, sword drill or brawling.
Choose a leader and team. Sword-drill participants must each have a weapon issued.
Participants still go to work or satisfy urgent needs; free-time practice never
overrides a job or need. Practice is abstract deck activity, not combat training
with a tactical resolution. F2/A/F4/F6 work as usual while underway.

NPC SOCIETY / SEA NEWS
Five persistent NPC vessels move on charted lanes: the Blackwake pirate fleet's
flagship and escort, two independent crews, and a Marine patrol. Named captains have
ambitions and reputations; organizations have leaders, relationships, alliances and
rivalries. Offscreen meetings can build trust or trigger abstract clashes. A clash
can damage a ship, alter reputations/relationships and force a retreat; this is
strategic NPC simulation, not player tactical combat. Named ships, their activity,
and recent causal news appear in V > sea chart / recent world news. The society,
relationships and news persist through saves. The trader buys/sells regional goods;
pirates hunt vessels and Marines pursue their rivals.

The prototype also models an NPC-only coercive slave market at
Freeport: raider-held captives can be sold and rival crews can buy from a finite
market pool. These transfers conserve tracked headcount; the player cannot trade,
own, or command enslaved people in this slice. This is a fictional system record,
not an endorsement. NPC trade and movement continue during normal in-game time,
in port or at sea.

CREW LIFE
Six named sailors have individual labor preferences, skill levels, assigned
bunks, hunger, thirst, fatigue, loneliness, boredom, health, morale and thoughts.
Energy is short-term stamina. A rest restores energy and physical fatigue, but
does not repay sleep debt: sailors must actually sleep in their assigned bunks.
Early birds and night owls prefer different sleeping hours. Urgent sleep debt
overrides those preferences. Needs/recovery rates are tuned for this prototype.

Personality dimensions (diligence, sociability, resilience and curiosity) affect
work interruptions, company/variety needs and stress. Traits have actual effects:
hearty eaters get hungry faster, light sleepers recover more slowly, shy sailors
seek company less often, hard workers work faster, perfectionists patch better
but work more slowly, and quick learners gain more practice experience.
F2 profiles explain each sailor's traits and show exact skills/practice totals.
Mood combines fading memories, unmet needs, stress and resilience. Ordinary work
may be refused when a sailor is miserable; survival/emergency chores take precedence.
They walk to stations, open doors, carry supplies and work without direct orders.
Urgent needs interrupt a chore; the exclusive claim and progress are retained.
Meals and water are finite. Missing supplies / blocked routes appear on the board.
Food and water deprivation eventually damage health and morale.
Sailors seek companionship, develop friendships, relax and enjoy the sea view.
You can speak with nearby crew to ease loneliness and improve morale.

CHORES
Hull repair, bilge bailing, fire suppression, meal preparation, swabbing, rigging and cannon loading.
Wear and low stocks create work automatically. No two sailors claim the same job.
Supplies are reserved in spatial stockpiles, physically collected, then carried to the site.
Fire suppression uses repair labor: workers haul one water unit to an active fire. Flames
damage actual wooden structure; smoke diffuses through open passages and stairs. Map markers
show `*` fire and `:` smoke, while the ship ledger lists active hazard counts.
The starter ship has movable medicine and brig fixtures. Crew below 80 health seek care only if the
medicine chest is accessible inside a room designated Infirmary; they walk to it and recover gradually.
Magazine reports inspect actual cannon stations and loaded charges; cannon-loading chores haul shot
as usual. Berths use real assigned bunks. Defeated boarders can be interned only if the brig is
designated, its cage installed, and all room boundary doors are locked; capacity is three. Enter >
Room designation / report offers captive release when prisoners are aboard. Feeding and negotiation
are not modeled yet.
Cancellation returns carried supplies by walking them back, not teleportation.
Cancelled chores have a two-minute requeue delay. Labor priorities are 0 = off,
1 = preferred through 5 = last resort; board priorities take precedence.
Select a chore in A and choose Assign to give it to a named sailor. Direct orders
override ordinary preferences, but never override disabled labors, urgent needs,
missing supplies or inaccessible work sites. Busy sailors finish their current
claim first. Reassigning active work returns materials before handing it over.

WHO IS FOLLOWING ORDERS?
F2 > Work compliance, or F6 > Work compliance, shows every sailor's live task,
order assignee, progress, needs-related pauses and explicit blockage reasons.
The report runs actual claim/labor/material consistency checks (not a fixed PASS).
F6 > Detailed work audit lists assignments, acceptance, material pickup, work
starts, need pauses/resumes, completions and supply returns with simulation seconds.

Time is TURN-BASED: menus never tick the simulation. Walking, waiting, transfers,
door actions and conversations advance it. Use F4 to fast-forward, or choose
Watch ship life for accelerated, animated observation (Esc stops watching).

EQUIPMENT RELOCATION
Enter -> Relocate equipment selects an existing fixture and a destination beside the captain.
Carpenters unfasten it, physically haul it along available routes, then refasten it.
Cyan F marks the planned destination. Heavy fixtures take longer per movement tile.
Equipment is unavailable while being hauled; it is removed from the source map, not duplicated.
Cancellation, reassignment or urgent needs return carried equipment to its original position first.
Mid-haul saves retain the carrier and exclusive claim. A manages these orders; F4 advances work.
Stocked hold relocation awaits spatial storage. Tackle/roller hauling speed is provisional;
multi-worker handling and structural foundations are not simulated yet.

ROOMS / DESIGNATIONS
Enter -> Room designation / report inspects or labels the space you stand in.
Walls and doors determine room boundaries; opening a door does not erase its room.
Labels are saved per tile, so splitting/merging spaces preserves their intended uses.
Assign berths, mess, galley, hold, workshop, infirmary, magazine, brig or working deck.
Reports show actual area, walkable tiles, bunks, stations, boundary doors and static hold-route
distances. Labels never create equipment or grant bonuses. Infirmary requires the movable medical
chest; magazine status reports actual cannon stations/ammo; brig captures defeated boarders only
with its cage, designation and locked boundary doors. Spaces are not watertight/roofed compartments.
Existing saves without designations start with undesignated spaces; equipment still works.

STOCKPILES / HAULING
Enter -> Storage / hauling inspects real pile positions, contents, available capacity and marks.
Designate/remove an empty floor stockpile beside the captain; designated stockpiles block walls.
Order physical transfers by selecting source, destination, resource and quantity.
Workers reserve the source, walk to collect and deliver it; cancellations return goods to source.
One load is at most 50 kg; heavier bundles must be moved in separate trips. Load adds travel delay.
Existing hold/water totals are backed by spatial piles; moving a stocked hold moves its contents,
adds their mass to the haul and makes the storage inaccessible until reinstalled.
Recipe inputs can be reserved from multiple reachable piles. Available totals exclude reservations
and carried goods; F6 audit reports double/missing allocations. Saves retain piles and mid-haul work.
Floor piles can be removed only empty and unreserved; fixtures and active-job routes are protected.

LOGISTICS / TRAFFIC INSPECTION
Enter -> Logistics / traffic inspector estimates active crew routes, door-opening and carried-load
travel costs, shared route tiles, occupied route cells, recent completed-job throughput, and nearest
repair-labor travel to fires/breaches. It is a snapshot, not a path reservation or guarantee: it ignores
future needs, changing traffic and work duration. Enable shared-route `;` marks to display projected
congestion on the current deck; hazard and lock markers take precedence. Clear the overlay from the
same menu. The inspector does not alter time or job state.

CONSTRUCTION / BLUEPRINTS
Enter -> Construction orders -> North/East/South/West selects an adjacent tile.
Build a connected deck floor, oak wall or door, or dismantle an interior barrier/floor.
Building costs one provisional plank bundle and uses the Patch hull labor skill.
Workers reserve and carry planks from the hold before doing the construction.
Cyan #/+ mark planned walls/doors; cyan x marks dismantling. Plans do not block walking.
Use A to inspect/prioritize/cancel construction and F4 to let the crew work.
Menus and designations do not advance time. Save/load retains work and carried supplies.
An occupied tile blocks completion until clear; blocked work retains its supplies.
Stations, bunks, essential access and the outer hull are protected in this slice.
Dismantling currently recovers no materials. F8 opens the ship-local blueprint editor;
arrows move the cursor, B cycles floor/wall/door/stair/beam/pillar build/dismantle, Space marks/unmarks, T sets
a drawing anchor, L draws a line, G a rectangle, F flood-fills within current deck bounds, and
Y toggles vertical-centerline symmetry. [ and ] set selection corners, D sets copy destination,
C copies, M mirrors, R rotates, Z undoes one gesture, Backspace clears all proposed edits, Enter
queues physical jobs, and Esc exits. Purple structural marks are nonphysical proposals until queued.
Copy/mirror/rotation and all proposed edits can be undone before job queueing. Floor removal
at the outer-hull edge or beneath walls is deliberately rejected. Flood-aware hull opening remains
future work. The F8 editor works on the selected deck; floors, barriers and
stair connectors can be planned and built as physical jobs. PageUp/PageDown changes the captain's
deck at a built stair, and crew can haul materials through stairs to upper-deck construction. Spatial
stockpiles and dropped supplies now retain their deck and can be hauled, picked up and saved upstairs.
Equipment can also be physically carried through stairs and installed on a clear upper-deck floor;
crew use the installed fixture's real elevated location. Some room reporting and ship-wide logistics
remain deck-zero, so multi-deck operations are still a partial simulation.
Blueprint jobs reject tiles crews cannot physically reach; connected floor extensions are queued
from the current deck outward and each section requires its own plank haul.
The ship ledger reports actual deck-zero floor area, exposed contour edges, enclosed cutouts,
connected sections, structural and categorized carried mass, center of mass, estimated displacement,
draft, freeboard, loading margin, trim, list and vertical stability. Geometry and actual cargo, stores,
actors, equipment, meals, ammunition and bilge load the estimate. It also flags unsupported floors,
beam spans and estimated overloads. Oak beams span up to four tiles; pillars strengthen vertical bearings.
Unsupported/overloaded structure fatigues over time and can fail; clear people, equipment, stockpiles
and dropped items from critically damaged floor sections so a collapse can detach them safely. These
are prototype ratings, not naval engineering calculations. Buoyancy uses a provisional 0.4 m map tile,
2 m design draft and seawater density. During a chart voyage, wind, rigging condition, sail drive,
resistance, mass-sensitive acceleration and rudder steering affect speed and route tracking. Choose
V > Set rudder angle to steer relative to the navigator's leg; the sea chart shows estimated speed,
heading, forces and cross-track drift. Voyage routes remain strategic chart legs; this is not direct
tile-by-tile sailing or a naval engineering simulator. Local breach (`!`), flood (`~`), fire (`*`)
and smoke (`:`) markers appear on deck. Water spreads slowly over unblocked nearby floors and through
open doors; crew bilge work pumps reachable water from the bilge station. Queue a wall build at a
breached hull tile for a physical patch. Fires damage wooden components and generate repair-labor
suppression jobs that haul real water. Rates are coarse prototype rules, not pressure, compartment,
combustion or ventilation physics.

Enter -> Toggle lock on nearby door / Engage ship lockdown bars doors across all built decks and
crew routes respect the locks. `L` marks a locked door. At a pirate-vessel sighting, V -> Resolve
nearby-ship sighting -> Repel a pirate boarding party starts a tactical fight on a projection of the
current built deck. Boarders breach a real outer hull wall, route toward the captain, and force/destroy
locked doors only when no alternate route is available. These changes and resulting flooding persist.
Win to continue the voyage; withdraw or be defeated to retreat. Boarding combat itself pauses ship time;
it does not yet track persistent wounds, captures or crew tactical allies.
